\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Training and model selection}
N-MNIST contains event recordings of handwritten digits \cite{orchard2015}. The hybrid campaign uses a frozen stratified partition of 55,000 training and 5,000 validation examples from the official training set. Architecture selection was performed with seed 42 and validation data. The chosen wide4 frontend and project\_measure2 circuit were then held fixed across model seeds 42, 123, 777, 2026 and 6543. The official test partition was not instantiated for the final QSNN campaign [R1]. Consequently, its reported clean accuracy is validation accuracy after model selection.

All five hybrid runs load the same hash-verified, seed-42 classical frontend checkpoint. The frontend is frozen for the first two epochs, then optimized with its BatchNorm layers kept in evaluation mode. The random seeds vary subsequent initialization and training stochasticity; they do not represent five independently pretrained frontends or five data splits. This conditional design limits the scope of the reported between-seed standard deviation.


Training uses AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$ and batch size 256 for at most 40 epochs. Learning rates are $5\times10^{-5}$ for the extractor, $2.5\times10^{-4}$ for the projection/circuit, and $10^{-3}$ for the classifier. A ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler uses factor 0.5, patience 2 and minimum rate $10^{-5}$. The highest validation accuracy selects a checkpoint, with validation loss breaking ties; early stopping uses patience 6 after at least eight epochs. Gradients are clipped at norm 1. The frontend uses float16 autocast; circuit inputs and amplitudes use float32 and complex64. Seed 42 was externally interrupted during epoch 38; its saved epoch-37 checkpoint was re-evaluated [R1].

\subsection{Populations and attack budgets}
The timestamp protocol freezes 100 validation examples, with ten examples per class, correctly classified by both models before attack. It reuses their identities across conditions. This joint-correct restriction permits paired attack comparisons but excludes examples that only one model classifies correctly. It is not a population-wide robust-accuracy estimate. The compact SNN checkpoint in this study differs from the retrained custom-SNN checkpoint in the controlled grid study.

PGD evaluates 21 candidate timestamps, including the clean candidate. Its implementation makes 41 attack-internal forward passes and 20 backward evaluations because candidate scoring and gradient computation use separate forwards. TEMP scores $Q$ proposed examples in batched model calls and uses no backwards. The first matched condition gives TEMP 21 candidates; the second matches 41 evaluated examples, not 41 batched Python model calls (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Neither converts a backward pass into an assumed number of forward passes. Verification adds two forwards per new record and is excluded from attack timing.

Elapsed-time calibration selects Q = 4,000 for QSNN and Q = 1,600 for SNN. The reported median TEMP/PGD timing ratios are 0.978 and 1.083, respectively [R3]. Candidate matching, forward matching and elapsed-time matching answer different computational questions. Exact equality of elapsed time is not asserted, and no throughput or latency claim is inferred from the ratios alone. The source synchronizes CUDA around timing, warms up PGD and TEMP, and uses ten disjoint training calibration examples (one per class), two repeats and $\epsilon_f=0.10$. TEMP evaluates candidate chunks of at most 64; PGD handles one example per forward. Timestamp tensors are float32 and circuit amplitudes complex64; training autocast settings are not assumed to describe attack timing. Saved timing arrays and a run-specific hardware/precision snapshot are unavailable. The supplement records calibration details and remaining provenance limits.

The controlled grid campaign trains four architectures under a common local recipe, data split, seed, optimizer settings and validation-selected checkpoint rule [R4]. For each representation it selects the first 1,000 official-test examples that all four models classify correctly. The joint-correct pools contain 9,522 binary and 9,495 integer examples. Within each representation the same frozen manifest is used for every architecture and budget. The two representations use different manifests. The public source snapshot contains the controlled-model loader, reference definitions, training and attack scripts. ConvNet denotes spiking \texttt{SimpleNet\_v2}, not the separate ReLU CNN class; classical here means nonquantum. The reference source files match the pinned upstream implementation. Exact reconstruction still requires the missing resolved training configurations, checkpoints, manifests and recorded hashes. Source availability does not establish replay of every historical result. The supplement records classes, aggregation and source hashes.

The recovered grid training script selects checkpoints by validation accuracy/loss but uses final test accuracy $\geq0.95$ to mark a run PASS and decide skip/revise actions. This creates a possible test-dependent recipe gate. Logs establishing whether that branch actually changed historical recipes are absent; test independence of recipe development therefore remains unverified. Fresh campaigns must freeze the recipe on validation and report test outcomes without a test-accuracy completion threshold, retaining validity, leakage and hash gates.

\subsection{Metrics, paired tests and evidence}
Clean accuracy uses the complete stated partition. ASR is the fraction of the stated clean-correct attack population that becomes misclassified. Thus $1-\mathrm{ASR}$ is survival under the evaluated search on that selected subset, not unconditional robust accuracy. Joint-correct selection improves within-study comparability but changes the estimand relative to all examples.

For the stated clean-correct attack population $\mathcal{P}$, we use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asr}
\operatorname{ASR}(\mathcal{P})=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[f(X_i')\neq c_i\right].
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:matched} uses 95\% Wilson score intervals \cite{wilson1927} computed from the reported numerator and $n=100$; the formula is given in the supplement. These are descriptive binomial-model intervals, with no sampling-based claim for the deterministically selected population or across reused conditions. They are not intervals for the paired difference. We report arithmetic means and sample standard deviations across the stated model seeds. For paired timestamp comparisons, $b$ counts examples broken only by PGD and $c$ counts examples broken only by TEMP. The exact two-sided McNemar framework \cite{mcnemar1947} uses a $\operatorname{Binomial}(b+c,1/2)$ null distribution, with $p=1$ for $b+c=0$. It assesses observed paired discordance, not equality of all possible attack strengths. Reused examples across seeds and conditions are not treated as independent additional subjects.

All numerical results are taken from preserved reports and source files at repository snapshot \texttt{80d02f5733d4} \cite{repository}; the full commit identifier is recorded in the supplement. We checked parameter arithmetic, aggregate statistics, paired counts and exact paired $p$-values. Model execution and per-example bootstrap resampling were not repeated. Missing raw outputs and provenance boundaries are documented in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} and the supplement.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
The primary timestamp experiment identifies feasible retimings that change QSNN-v3 predictions. Its numerical PGD/TEMP contrast changes with computational matching: the two extra failures at the larger radius occur under elapsed-time calibration and disappear under access matching. The paired tests remain inconclusive. The classical controls answer a separate question: similar clean accuracy need not imply similar outcomes under the same nominal attack recipe. Different victims, samples and budget units prevent pooling the two studies.

\paragraph{Mechanism and encoding.}
The quantum circuit is one stage of an attacked composition. Timestamp changes can affect bin counts, spiking summaries, angles and measurement probabilities before the affine readout. Without fixed-frontend classical-head and circuit ablations, failures cannot be attributed uniquely to quantum processing. The margin observation explains why state displacement alone need not change a label, but final-model per-example states and margins are missing. Historical Iris diagnostics in the supplement do not supply that evidence. Encoding- and access-specific quantum analyses \cite{wendlinger2024comparative,gong2024randomized,dowling2026guarantees} consequently provide context rather than guarantees for our temporal feasible set.

\paragraph{Search strength.}
Low or zero ASR can reflect optimizer limitations. PGD uses one start and 20 steps, with cross-entropy-based output selection; TEMP uses a finite margin search. The large classical architecture gap requires gradient, preprocessing and search-strength diagnostics before interpretation as an intrinsic robustness difference. Alternative surrogates, restarts and derivative-free budget curves should be checked with valid hard-forward candidates \cite{athalye2018obfuscated,croce2020reliable}. Image-space attack ensembles and defenses, including classical distillation of quantum manipulations \cite{khatun2025distillation}, require adaptation to event constraints.

\paragraph{Evaluation and evidence boundary.}
Five hybrid seeds share pretraining and split, while attacks use one victim seed and a selected validation population. Official-test QSNN robustness, independently pretrained pipelines and a larger attack population remain unmeasured. The populated comparison report conflicts with its README's statement that results are absent; we use the report and disclose that inconsistency. Most checkpoints, raw arrays, manifests and logs referenced by reports are missing. Our arithmetic and static code checks do not reproduce model runs or independently repeat their attributed audits. The grid penalty ambiguity and incomplete baseline provenance remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Next empirical steps.}
After validation-based model selection, evaluate QSNN clean and attacked performance on the official test set across independently trained frontend/head seeds, retaining sample IDs and clean errors. Sweep steps, step size, restarts, cross-entropy/margin losses and surrogate choices, recording the best valid success by budget and reconfirming it with hard forwards. Hold frontend, split and training budget fixed for capacity-matched classical heads and circuit variants with/without entanglement, including parameter counts and training cost. To test the state/margin interpretation, retain per-example states, probabilities and margins and control clean margin and realized distortion; mean fidelity alone would not establish a mechanism.

A future monitor must specify eight-qubit observables, shot budget, clean-only calibration and response policy before measuring ROC/PR, false-positive rates and adaptive attacks. Single-qubit marginals do not determine the full state. Event-count preservation is also distinct from stealth: no completed statistical-indistinguishability or final-model detection/defense evaluation exists. The historical corrected grid campaign remains only 8 of 33 conditions complete.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Computational matching changes the numerical attack comparison on the implemented QSNN-v3, but none of the paired conditions establishes TEMP superiority. Constrained retiming changes 3--5 of 100 examined joint-correct validation predictions. Separate classical controls show why clean accuracy and a common recipe are insufficient summaries of attack behavior. The resulting evidence is specific to the stated protocols; independent official-test hybrid evaluation is the principal missing validation.

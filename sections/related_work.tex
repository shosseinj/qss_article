\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Raw-event attacks and constrained retiming.}
Surrogate-gradient learning enables optimization through spiking dynamics \cite{neftci2019}. Event additions/removals \cite{buchel2022attacks} and raw-event value/support attacks \cite{yao2024rawevent} have different feasible sets from identity-preserving retiming. Configurable-latency attacks provide a closer temporal connection \cite{du2026latencies}. Yu et al. formalize capacity-one retiming with maximum displacement, total delay and retimed-spike budgets \cite{yu2026}; our packet-grid controls use a local implementation. Native timestamp retiming instead permits frame-cell collisions and uses duration-scaled radii, so the two regimes require separate interpretation.

\paragraph{Surrogates and temporal robustness in SNNs.}
Lun et al. study inference-invisible surrogate gradients and propose potential-dependent surrogates and sparse attacks \cite{lun2025surrogate}. Robust temporal self-ensembling \cite{wang2026rte} and sparse ANN-to-SNN conversion \cite{schmolli2025sparse} examine training and construction choices affecting adversarial behavior. These motivate architecture-aware optimization diagnostics, with perturbation sets and training interventions distinct from timing-only evaluation.

\paragraph{Adversarial access and derivative-free search.}
PGD is a standard iterative baseline \cite{madry2018}. Query-limited, partial-information and label-only attacks expose different interfaces \cite{ilyas2018queries}. TEMP uses logits and the true label without backward gradients: it is a score-based, not label-only, attack. Random-search precedents include Square Attack \cite{andriushchenko2020square}, although its image-space proposals do not directly respect temporal feasibility. Robustness evaluation \cite{carlini2019}, obfuscated-gradient analysis \cite{athalye2018obfuscated}, diverse attack ensembles \cite{croce2020reliable} and adaptive attacks \cite{tramer2020adaptive} motivate checking the implemented pipeline beyond one optimizer.

\paragraph{Hybrid spiking and quantum models.}
Brand and Petruccione develop quantum LIF neurons \cite{brand2024}; Jha et al. use SNN-derived EEG features with quantum kernels \cite{jha2025hybrid}; SPATE investigates spike-derived temporal encodings \cite{spate2026}. QSNN-v3 instead combines classical Conv/LIF summaries, learned angles, a data-re-uploading circuit \cite{perez2020} and an affine classifier on joint probabilities. The temporal features originate in the classical frontend; the circuit does not directly implement raw-event quantum LIF dynamics.

\paragraph{Quantum vulnerability, encoding and guarantees.}
Quantum adversarial learning considers classical and quantum input manipulations \cite{lu2020quantumadv}, while high-dimensional vulnerability analysis emphasizes state-space geometry \cite{liu2020vulnerability}. Encoding gates constrain variational expressivity \cite{schuld2021encoding}. Comparative quantum/classical attacks \cite{wendlinger2024comparative}, randomized encodings \cite{gong2024randomized} and quantum robustness guarantees \cite{dowling2026guarantees} depend on encoding and access assumptions. Certifiably robust encodings explicitly connect classical inputs, noise and classifiers \cite{saxena2024encoding}. Classical autoencoder distillation of quantum manipulations is another distinct intervention \cite{khatun2025distillation}. Our fixed-readout margin observation lacks the additional retiming-to-state bound needed for a timestamp certificate. The supplement compares these interfaces without ranking external ASRs.

{\LARGE\bfseries Temporal Retiming Attacks on\\[3pt]
Hybrid Quantum Spiking Classifiers\par}
\vspace{9pt}
{\normalsize Author names withheld for review\par}
\vspace{12pt}
\end{center}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\small\textbf{Abstract.} Retiming can alter an event-driven classifier while preserving event identities, coordinates, polarity and count. We study this attack surface in an implemented hybrid quantum spiking neural network and a separate controlled classical experiment. QSNN-v3 combines a convolutional leaky integrate-and-fire frontend with an eight-qubit data re-uploading circuit and a learned probability readout. Across five training seeds it reaches 97.272\% $\pm$ 0.212\% validation accuracy, conditional on one pretrained frontend and one split. On 100 jointly clean-correct seed-42 validation examples, timestamp PGD succeeds on three QSNN examples at both 5\% and 10\% duration budgets. Derivative-free TEMP-DRIFT-v2 succeeds on four and three under candidate or forward-pass matching, and four and five under calibrated elapsed-time matching. No paired comparison establishes significant TEMP superiority. In a separate ten-bin packet-retiming study on 1,000 jointly clean-correct official-test examples per representation, the custom SNN has 98.0--100\% attack success, whereas three locally trained controls have 0--1.9\% under the two completed budgets. The results identify timing-only vulnerability and show why computational matching and temporal representation are necessary for interpreting observed failure rates. The quantum circuit is simulated analytically; official-test hybrid robustness and tomography-based defenses remain open empirical questions.
\par\vspace{5pt}\textbf{Keywords:} event-driven classification; adversarial retiming; hybrid quantum classifiers; spiking neural networks; matched attack evaluation.
\end{minipage}
